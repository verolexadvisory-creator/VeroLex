Family law

A Calm and Fair Resolution of Family Matters

Family disputes are always personal and sensitive. Relying on the Family Code and the Civil Code of the Republic of Uzbekistan, we protect the client's interests and above all those of the children, seeking a pre-trial settlement wherever possible.

§ 01

Divorce

Dissolving a marriage through the civil registry or in court, drafting the statement of claim and representation at hearings.

§ 02

Division of Property

Dividing spouses' joint property, separating personal assets and formalising a division agreement.

§ 03

Alimony Matters

Recovering alimony as a share of income or a fixed sum, changing the amount and collecting arrears.

§ 04

Child-Related Disputes

Determining a child's place of residence, contact arrangements with the other parent, establishing paternity and adoption.

§ 05

Inheritance

Formalising acceptance of an inheritance, restoring missed deadlines, compulsory shares and disputes among heirs.

§ 06

Wills and Prenuptial Agreements

Drafting wills and prenuptial agreements, notarial certification, and proceedings to have them declared invalid.

Legal basis

The Family Code, the Civil Code (Section V — Inheritance Law) and the Civil Procedural Code of the Republic of Uzbekistan, together with the Supreme Court Plenum rulings on divorce and inheritance. Advice is given based on the specific circumstances of each family.

The most common questions on family law

Where the spouses agree, have no minor children in common and there is no dispute over property or maintenance, the marriage is dissolved at the civil registry. Otherwise the case is heard in court.

Absent an agreement between the parents, the court awards 1/4 of monthly earnings and other income for one child, 1/3 for two and 1/2 for three or more. The amount per child may not be less than 26.5 per cent of the statutory minimum wage.

Property acquired during the marriage is treated as joint and is usually divided equally. The court may depart from equal shares in the interests of minor children or other relevant circumstances. Property owned before the marriage or received by inheritance or gift is personal property.

The certificate of the right to inheritance is issued six months after the inheritance opens, and a notary must be approached within that period. If the deadline is missed for a valid reason, it may be restored by the court.

Arrears are collected through enforcement proceedings; the bailiff may restrict property and accounts and temporarily bar travel abroad. Prolonged non-payment may result in administrative and criminal liability.
